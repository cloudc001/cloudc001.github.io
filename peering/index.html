A selective, practical approach

Research value and reliable operation

How we evaluate requests

Requests are reviewed individually. We consider a useful routing relationship, a suitable interconnection location, and the ability to operate the session responsibly. There is no fixed traffic-ratio requirement.

A contract is not normally required. Selective peering means that a request or shared exchange presence does not automatically establish a peering relationship.

Interconnection options

We consider IPv4 and IPv6 unicast sessions. Bilateral sessions and route-server participation are considered where available; the location, transport, address families, and limits are agreed before activation.

Please use our PeeringDB record for published exchange presence and interface details. An Anycast PoP or upstream location does not by itself imply an available public peering port.

Route exchange

  • Our public peering exports are limited to our agreed prefixes originated by AS203991. We do not offer third-party transit or advertise downstream customer cones.
  • Announce only routes you are authorized to originate or export. Default routes, full tables, and third-party transit are not part of an ordinary peering session.
  • Keep IRR route objects and ROAs accurate where available. We expect valid origin authorization; RPKI-invalid routes are not acceptable for peering.
  • Exchange an explicit route scope and set max-prefix limits per session. Filter private, reserved, and otherwise unauthorized routes.

Session operation

  • Use a valid public ASN and keep a reachable operations contact and current PeeringDB information.
  • Agree on neighbor addresses, address families, transport, and any session authentication before enabling BGP. Exchange secrets privately.
  • Avoid route leaks and unnecessary update churn. Notify us of persistent session faults or unexpected announcements.
  • Multicast is not currently supported. This educational network operates on a best-effort basis, with no commercial SLA.
Geofeed is separate from route export. Geographic labels for smaller address blocks do not authorize additional BGP announcements. Only the prefixes explicitly agreed for a session should be accepted.

Anycast and network boundaries

Public routing stays separate from the lab

Multiple paths, one origin

Some public prefixes are announced from multiple locations. The same destination may reach different sites depending on the source network and current BGP policy.

Anycast does not guarantee the geographically closest site, symmetric paths, or a particular latency. When reporting an issue, include both the source network and a timestamp so we can identify the path involved.

DN42 is a separate relationship

Public AS203991 sessions are separate from our DN42 overlay. DN42 routes, private AS paths, and lab address space must not be exported into public Internet sessions; public transit must not be provided through the DN42 lab.

For overlay peering, use dn42.cloudc.dev. A public peering agreement does not establish a DN42 session, or vice versa.
